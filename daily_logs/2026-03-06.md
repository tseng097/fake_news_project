## Papers (new-to-this-repo shortlist)

### 1) R-Drop: Regularized Dropout for Neural Networks
- Citation: Liang, Chen, and Xu. **“R-Drop: Regularized Dropout for Neural Networks.”** arXiv:2106.14448, 2021.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14448
- Core method: Do **two stochastic forward passes** with dropout; minimize task loss plus **symmetric KL** between the two predictive distributions.
- Assumptions:
  - Dropout noise approximates small, label-preserving perturbations.
  - Agreement under dropout encourages smoother decision boundaries.
- Strengths:
  - Very cheap vs full adversarial training; no external augmenter needed.
  - Often improves calibration/stability; complements text augmentations.
- Limitations:
  - Robustness gains depend on dropout actually being active and sufficiently strong.
  - Doesn’t explicitly target lexical/style/sentiment attacks; more “generic smoothness.”
- Worth-Reading score (for *this* project): **84/100**

### 2) SMART: Robust and Efficient Fine-Tuning for Pre-trained Language Models via Adversarial and Contrastive Learning
- Citation: Jiang et al. **“SMART: Robust and Efficient Fine-Tuning for Pre-trained Language Models.”** arXiv:2004.06277, 2020.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06277
- Core method: Add a **small worst-case perturbation** to embeddings (adversarial regularization) to enforce local smoothness; optionally add contrastive objectives.
- Assumptions:
  - Local smoothness around the input representation improves downstream robustness.
- Strengths:
  - Stronger than dropout-only regularization; targets adversarial fragility directly.
- Limitations:
  - More implementation complexity (perturbation steps, normalization); adds compute.
  - Not explicitly about fake news; must be adapted/evaluated.
- Worth-Reading score: **78/100**

### 3) Learning the Difference that Makes a Difference with Counterfactually-Augmented Data
- Citation: Kaushik, H. et al. **“Learning the Difference that Makes a Difference with Counterfactually-Augmented Data.”** (CAD), arXiv:1909.12434, 2019.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12434
- Core method: Create **counterfactual edits** that flip labels while keeping most of the text fixed; train to reduce reliance on spurious cues.
- Assumptions:
  - Human/controlled counterfactual edits reflect causal features.
- Strengths:
  - Directly aligns with “adversarial reframing” and robustness to superficial cues.
  - Conceptually fits style/sentiment reframes and can guide evaluation.
- Limitations:
  - High-quality counterfactual generation is hard (often requires humans or strong generators).
  - For LIAR-style statements, label-flipping edits may be subtle and domain-specific.
- Worth-Reading score: **70/100**

## Synthesis vs current `fake_news_project`
Current repo robustness methods:
- **Text-level perturbations**: WordNet synonym swaps (lexical), contraction/punctuation normalization (style), lexicon swaps (sentiment).
- **Training objective**: CE on clean + **one-way KL**(clean || augmented) consistency.

Gap addressed today:
- No robustness regularizer when augmentations are weak/noisy/unavailable.
- No “in-distribution stochastic consistency” (dropout) baseline.

Proposed alignment:

## Professor-style discussion questions
1. For fake news detection, does enforcing **dropout-consistency** (R-Drop) reduce reliance on stylistic shortcuts, or mainly improve calibration without true invariance?
2. How should we weigh **text-level invariances** (synonyms/style/sentiment) vs **representation-level smoothness** (SMART/FreeLB/R-Drop) when robustness threats are mostly lexical and pragmatic?
3. If synonym substitution sometimes changes truth-conditions (non-label-preserving), what objective best tolerates *noisy invariance*—KL consistency, contrastive losses, or selective consistency based on semantic similarity?
4. Would CAD-style counterfactuals for LIAR statements tend to flip labels by changing *facts* (entities/numbers) rather than *style*—and does that undermine the “robustness” framing?
5. What robustness metric best matches fake news risk: average attacked accuracy drop, **ASR**, worst-case over attack families, or expected risk under a realistic attack distribution?

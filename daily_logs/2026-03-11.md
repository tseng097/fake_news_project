## Papers (new to this repo log)

### Paper A
- **Citation:** Flores, L. J., & Hao, K. (2022). *An Adversarial Benchmark for Fake News Detection Models*. AAAI Workshop on Adversarial ML and Beyond. arXiv:2201.00912.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00912
- **Core method:** Builds adversarial evaluation sets for fake-news detectors targeting (i) compositional semantics, (ii) lexical relations, and (iii) modifier sensitivity.
- **Assumptions:** Label should remain stable under controlled lexical/compositional rewrites; benchmark perturbations approximate realistic adversarial edits.
- **Strengths:** Directly probes lexical and modifier brittleness; highly aligned with robustness evaluation needs.
- **Limitations:** Benchmark-centric (evaluation), not a full training framework; focused on tested datasets/models.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **93/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Ren, Y., Zhang, Y., Zhang, M., Yu, D., Zhang, Y., & Qu, Q. (2021). *Adversarial Active Learning based Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network for Fake News Detection*. ICDM 2020. arXiv:2101.11206.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11206
- **Core method:** Uses adversarial selector + active learning over heterogeneous graph entities to prioritize informative samples for fake-news detection.
- **Assumptions:** News-item context graph (creator/subject/social relations) is available and helps detection; active querying mitigates low-label settings.
- **Strengths:** Practical for label-scarce setups; adversarial sample selection improves data efficiency.
- **Limitations:** Graph-heavy assumptions are outside this repo’s current text-only pipeline; less direct for lexical/style/sentiment perturbation mechanics.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **72/100**

### Paper C
- **Citation:** Jin, Z., Jin, D., Zhou, J. T., & Szolovits, P. (2020). *Is BERT Really Robust? A Strong Baseline for Natural Language Attack on Text Classification and Entailment (TextFooler)*. AAAI 2020. arXiv:1907.11932.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11932
- **Core method:** Word-level synonym substitution attack maximizing misclassification while preserving semantics and fluency.
- **Assumptions:** Word substitutions can preserve human-level semantics but still flip model prediction.
- **Strengths:** Foundational lexical attack recipe; directly relevant to `lexical_mhc_lite` perturbation quality and safety checks.
- **Limitations:** General NLP benchmark focus (not fake-news-specific); synonym filtering still imperfect in factual domains.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **90/100**

## Concise synthesis vs current methods
- Current project already has lexical/style/sentiment augmentation lanes with mHC-lite regularization.
- Paper A highlights a concrete blind spot not fully guarded in naive synonym swaps: **modifier/compositional pivots** (e.g., "only", "never").
- Paper C reinforces that lexical perturbations are useful but can be semantically risky without safeguards.
- Today’s enhancement therefore tightened lexical augmentation safety for mHC-lite by protecting modifier pivots, improving label-preservation likelihood without changing strategy scope.

### Paper A
- **Citation:** Shang, J., et al. (2025). **SARC: Sentiment-Augmented Deep Role Clustering for Fake News Detection**. arXiv:2511.04692.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04692
- **Core method:** Jointly encodes comment semantics + sentiment, then performs differentiable deep role clustering; fake-news detection is optimized together with role-cluster supervision.
- **Assumptions:** User-role signals and sentiment behavior in comments are stable enough to improve veracity cues.
- **Strengths:** Explicitly models sentiment as a structured signal instead of a flat auxiliary feature.
- **Limitations:** Not an explicit adversarial training framework; relies on comment availability/quality.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **82/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Zhou, W., et al. (2025). **Disentangling Fact from Sentiment: A Dynamic Conflict-Consensus Framework for Multimodal Fake News Detection**. arXiv:2512.20670.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20670
- **Core method:** Decouples fact vs sentiment spaces, then amplifies cross-modal contradictions via a conflict-consensus dynamic.
- **Assumptions:** Fabrication evidence is often in contradiction patterns that standard consistency fusion suppresses.
- **Strengths:** Strong framing for separating factual evidence from emotional style/sentiment confounds.
- **Limitations:** Multimodal design; transfer to text-only pipelines is indirect.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **79/100**

### Paper C
- **Citation:** Li, L., et al. (2020). **BERT-Attack: Adversarial Attack Against BERT Using BERT**. EMNLP 2020. arXiv:2004.09984.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09984
- **Core method:** Uses masked-LM substitutions to produce fluent, semantically close lexical adversarial examples with high attack success.
- **Assumptions:** High-importance lexical tokens can be perturbed while retaining human-perceived semantics.
- **Strengths:** Practical lexical attack recipe directly relevant to mHC-lite synonym robustness.
- **Limitations:** General NLP benchmark focus, not fake-news-specific datasets.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **90/100**

## 2) Concise synthesis vs current fake_news_project methods

- Current project already has a strong strategy lock and implementation mapping:
  - `lexical_mhc_lite`: synonym perturbation + symmetric KL consistency
  - `style_invariance`: style normalization/reframing
  - `sentiment_invariance`: controlled sentiment shift + JS/KL consistency
- From today’s papers:
  - SARC + DCCF reinforce that sentiment signals can confound veracity modeling if not isolated.
  - BERT-Attack reinforces that lexical perturbations hit high-importance words; in fake-news settings those often include named entities tied to factual claims.
- Practical implication adopted today: harden lexical mHC-lite augmentation by preserving likely named entities so lexical perturbation targets style/wording variation instead of factual-entity corruption.

## 3) Code improvement (small, meaningful, testable)

### Change implemented
- **File:** `src/augment.py`
- Added `_looks_like_named_entity(tokens, idx)` and integrated it into `lexical_synonym_perturb(...)` candidate selection.
- Effect: mid-sentence TitleCase likely entities (e.g., person/org/location names) are excluded from synonym replacement in the mHC-lite lexical path.

### Why this is paper-grounded
- BERT-Attack highlights lexical substitution attacks preferentially manipulating high-impact tokens.
- For fake-news detection, entity tokens are often factual anchors; replacing them can induce label-semantic drift and weaken consistency training quality.

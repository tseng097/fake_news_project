### Paper A
- **Citation:** Lucas, J., Murtagh-White, M., Uchendu, A., Al-Lawati, A., Yamashita, M., Macko, D., et al. (2026). **BLUFF: Benchmarking the Detection of False and Synthetic Content across 58 Low-Resource Languages**. arXiv:2603.00634.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00634
- **Core method:** Introduces a multilingual robustness benchmark (79 languages, 202k+ samples) spanning human/LLM/translated/hybrid content with many manipulation tactics and edit intensities.
- **Assumptions:** Robust fake-news detectors should generalize across language-resource settings and manipulation types.
- **Strengths:** Strong benchmark framing for distribution-shift robustness and manipulation diversity.
- **Limitations:** Benchmark-focused (not a direct training strategy recipe for this codebase); multilingual scope may exceed current project setup.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **78/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Li, D., Zhang, Y., Peng, H., Chen, L., Brockett, C., Sun, M.-T., & Dolan, B. (2021). **Contextualized Perturbation for Textual Adversarial Attack (CLARE)**. NAACL 2021. arXiv:2009.07502.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07502
- **Core method:** Context-aware adversarial edits (replace/insert/merge) with mask-then-infill to preserve fluency while increasing attack strength.
- **Assumptions:** Contextual perturbations produce stronger and more natural adversarial samples than static synonym heuristics.
- **Strengths:** Directly relevant to lexical robustness quality (semantic and grammatical plausibility of perturbations).
- **Limitations:** Primarily attack generation; does not directly solve fake-news-specific supervision issues.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **89/100**

### Paper C
- **Citation:** Garg, S., & Ramakrishnan, G. (2020). **BAE: BERT-based Adversarial Examples for Text Classification**. EMNLP 2020. arXiv:2004.01970.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01970
- **Core method:** Black-box adversarial generation using contextual BERT masking and replace/insert operations for higher semantic coherence.
- **Assumptions:** Contextualized candidate generation improves realism and attack transferability over raw synonym swaps.
- **Strengths:** Practical evidence that context-sensitive lexical edits are more robust stress tests than naive substitutions.
- **Limitations:** General text-classification focus; adapting full BERT-based attacker into this lightweight project would increase complexity.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **86/100**

## 2) Concise synthesis vs current fake_news_project methods

- Current project uses scope-locked strategies: `vanilla`, `lexical_mhc_lite`, `style_invariance`, `sentiment_invariance`.
- CLARE/BAE both reinforce a key weakness of plain synonym substitution: lexical edits can become ungrammatical/out-of-context and introduce training noise.
- For this project’s lightweight mHC-lite path, a practical takeaway is to add conservative morphology compatibility checks so lexical perturbations stay semantically and grammatically closer without adding new strategies.
- BLUFF reinforces the need to measure robustness under varied manipulations/distribution shifts; this informs evaluation planning but does not change strategy scope.

## 3) Code improvement (small, meaningful, testable)

### Change implemented
- **File:** `src/augment.py`
- Added `_morph_compatible(source, replacement)` and applied it inside `lexical_synonym_perturb(...)` lemma filtering.
- The guard preserves inflectional shape (e.g., `-ing`, `-ed`, `-ly`, plural `-s`) and blocks extreme token-length jumps.

### Why this is paper-grounded
- CLARE/BAE emphasize context-aware, fluent lexical perturbations; naive token swaps are often unnatural.
- This lightweight compatibility filter is a low-cost proxy to improve lexical perturbation quality in `lexical_mhc_lite` without introducing any new training strategy.

### Paper A
- **Citation:** Koenders, C., Filla, J., Schneider, N., & Woloszyn, V. (2021). **How Vulnerable Are Automatic Fake News Detection Methods to Adversarial Attacks?** arXiv:2107.07970.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07970
- **Core method:** Trains fake-news detectors and applies automated adversarial text attacks (via TextAttack) to flip predictions while preserving meaning.
- **Assumptions:** Attackers can craft minor lexical/character edits that keep human meaning intact but exploit detector shortcuts.
- **Strengths:** Directly demonstrates practical fragility of fake-news detectors under textual perturbation; highly relevant to robustness-focused training.
- **Limitations:** Older model ecosystem; attack setup is mostly lexical/character level and not explicitly style/sentiment disentanglement.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **88/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Przybyła, P., Shvets, A., & Saggion, H. (2023/2024). **Verifying the Robustness of Automatic Credibility Assessment.** arXiv:2303.08032 (later journal version in NLP 2025).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08032
- **Core method:** Systematic robustness testing for credibility classifiers with meaning-preserving character/word attacks; introduces BODEGA benchmark across fact-checking, hyperpartisan, propaganda, and rumour tasks.
- **Assumptions:** Real moderation settings involve adaptive adversaries making small edits around vulnerable spans.
- **Strengths:** Evaluation framing is close to deployment reality; includes multiple misinformation task families and manual adversarial-example analysis.
- **Limitations:** Benchmark-heavy; not a drop-in training recipe, and broader credibility scope is wider than this project’s strict strategy lock.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **91/100**

### Paper C
- **Citation:** Chakraborty, A., Khatri, I., Choudhry, A., Gupta, P., Vishwakarma, D. K., & Prasad, M. (2022). **An Emotion-guided Approach to Domain Adaptive Fake News Detection using Adversarial Learning.** arXiv:2211.17108.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17108
- **Core method:** Multi-task, adversarial domain-adaptation setup that injects emotion signals for cross-domain fake-news detection.
- **Assumptions:** Emotion/sentiment cues are informative but distribution-sensitive; adaptation helps transfer across domains.
- **Strengths:** Supports sentiment-aware robustness motivation and cross-domain invariance concerns.
- **Limitations:** Short paper/student track; limited implementation detail and narrower empirical depth than full conference papers.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **74/100**

## 2) Concise synthesis vs current fake_news_project methods

- Current scope-locked strategies remain: `vanilla`, `lexical_mhc_lite`, `style_invariance`, `sentiment_invariance`.
- Koenders et al. and Przybyła et al. both emphasize that **small meaning-preserving edits near vulnerable spans** can flip predictions; this supports conservative lexical guards in `lexical_mhc_lite`.
- Emotion-guided adaptation paper reinforces that sentiment cues can be brittle across distribution shift, supporting continued isolation of sentiment-specific perturbations inside `sentiment_invariance` rather than leaking them into lexical perturbation.
- Net takeaway: incremental robustness gains should come from **higher-quality perturbation controls** and **targeted invariance regularization**, not adding new strategies.

## 3) Code improvement (small, meaningful, testable)

### Change implemented
- **File:** `src/augment.py`
- Added two helpers and integrated them into `lexical_synonym_perturb(...)`:
  - `_is_numeric_token(tok)`
  - `_near_numeric_context(tokens, idx)`
- New guardrail: in `lexical_mhc_lite`, lexical substitutions are skipped for words adjacent to number-like tokens (e.g., `5 million`, `12%`) to reduce accidental factual claim drift.

### Why this is paper-grounded
- Robustness-verification papers above show attacks often exploit tiny local edits in vulnerable, high-information spans.
- Quantity-adjacent words are frequent claim anchors in fake-news text; preserving them keeps lexical augmentation focused on stylistic/paraphrastic variation instead of factual distortion.

### mHC-related comments/docstrings
- Added explicit mHC-lite docstrings/comments describing why numeric-context anchors are protected and how this maps to robustness findings.

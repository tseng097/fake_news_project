## 1) New paper scan (2, in-scope)

### Paper A
- **Citation:** Lai, Y., Ji, Y., & Du, Y. (2025). *ContrastFake: Combating LLM-Style Attacks with Supervised Contrastive Learning for Fake News Detection*. 2025 4th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence, Internet of Things and Cloud Computing Technology (AIoTC). DOI: 10.1109/AIoTC66747.2025.11198804.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11198804
- **Core method:** Learns style-invariant content representations with supervised contrastive objectives so veracity prediction remains stable under LLM-driven style mimicry.
- **Assumptions:** Style can be disentangled from veracity-bearing content; paired clean/style-attacked samples are available or can be generated.
- **Strengths:** Very aligned with `style_invariance`; explicitly targets LLM style attacks and spurious style shortcuts.
- **Limitations:** Contrastive pipelines usually add batch-structure and sampling complexity beyond this repo’s lightweight training loop.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **90/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Koren, C., Przybyła, P., et al. (2025). *Evaluating the robustness of fake news detectors to adversarial attacks with real user comments*. International Journal of Data Science and Analytics. DOI: 10.1007/s41060-025-00790-3.
- **Link:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-025-00790-3
- **Core method:** Proposes realistic comment-based attacks using real human-written comments (topic-specific and generic retrieval) to flip detector decisions.
- **Assumptions:** Comment/context channels are manipulable and often under-protected versus article-body perturbation defenses.
- **Strengths:** Uses realistic pre-existing comments rather than easy-to-detect synthetic text; strong external-validity signal for robustness evaluation.
- **Limitations:** Focuses on comment-aware detectors; transfer to text-only pipelines is indirect.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **82/100**

## 2) Concise synthesis vs current fake_news_project methods
- Current project already has dedicated invariance paths for lexical/style/sentiment perturbations.
- Paper A strengthens the case that style artifacts remain a primary attack surface under LLM rewriting; our style/sentiment augmentations should include social-text cues, not only formal news tone markers.
- Paper B highlights that human-authored social cues (slang/emotive phrasing) can be effective adversarial channels; this maps directly to expanding sentiment perturbation coverage for colloquial cues.

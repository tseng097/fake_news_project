## 1) New paper scan (3, in-scope)

### Paper A
- **Citation:** Li, L., Ma, R., Guo, Q., Xue, X., & Qiu, X. (2020). *BERT-ATTACK: Adversarial Attack Against BERT Using BERT*. EMNLP 2020.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09984
- **Core method:** Uses masked-language-model contextual substitutions to generate fluent adversarial lexical variants that preserve grammaticality while flipping predictions.
- **Assumptions:** Contextual MLM candidates are semantically close enough to preserve label intent; model gradients/scores can guide important token replacement.
- **Strengths:** Strong lexical attack realism compared with static synonym swaps; exposes brittleness to contextual paraphrases.
- **Limitations:** Attack-focused (not defense directly); transfer to fake-news label preservation requires additional semantic safeguards.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **88/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Li, D., Zhang, Y., Peng, H., Miao, L., & Wang, P. (2021). *Contextualized Perturbation for Textual Adversarial Attack (CLARE)*. NAACL-HLT 2021 Findings.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07502
- **Core method:** Context-aware replace/insert/merge perturbations with language-model scoring for fluency and stronger black-box attack effectiveness.
- **Assumptions:** Local contextual operations approximate natural edits better than isolated word-level swaps.
- **Strengths:** Highlights why context-free lexical perturbations can drift semantics/fluency; useful guidance for safer mHC-lite lexical augmentation constraints.
- **Limitations:** Includes operations (insert/merge) outside current repo’s lightweight WordNet-only augmentation approach.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **84/100**

### Paper C
- **Citation:** Garg, S., & Ramakrishnan, G. (2020). *BAE: BERT-based Adversarial Examples for Text Classification*. EMNLP 2020.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01970
- **Core method:** Generates adversarial examples via BERT-driven token replacement and insertion while maintaining readability and high attack success.
- **Assumptions:** Contextual candidate generation better preserves linguistic acceptability than naive synonym replacement.
- **Strengths:** Strong evidence that lexical robustness depends on context sensitivity and ambiguity control.
- **Limitations:** Heavier than this project’s no-LLM/no-MLM augmentation design.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **81/100**

## 2) Synthesis vs current fake_news_project methods
- Current `lexical_mhc_lite` relies on WordNet synonyms plus conservative safeguards (entity, quantity-adjacent, stance/hedge/discourse pivots, semantic similarity floor).
- BERT-ATTACK/CLARE/BAE reinforce a key risk: context-free substitutions are weakest on ambiguous high-polysemy words.
- Therefore, the best low-complexity in-scope improvement is to further tighten WordNet lexical substitutions on ambiguous source tokens, while keeping the same strategy set and training loop simplicity.

## 1) New paper scan (3, in-scope)

> Web limitation note: direct web search endpoint returned bot-detection challenge, so I used arXiv API fetch as the available source path.

### Paper A
- **Citation:** Tahmasebi, S., Müller-Budack, E., & Ewerth, R. (2026). *Robust Fake News Detection using Large Language Models under Adversarial Sentiment Attacks*. arXiv:2601.15277.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15277
- **Core method:** Introduces controlled sentiment adversarial attacks and a sentiment-agnostic consistency training framework (AdSent) to stabilize fake-news predictions under sentiment manipulation.
- **Assumptions:** Sentiment cues are exploitable shortcuts in fake-news detectors; enforcing sentiment-invariant veracity decisions improves robustness/generalization.
- **Strengths:** Directly aligned with this project’s `sentiment_invariance`; explicit attack-vs-defense framing; cross-scenario robustness evaluation.
- **Limitations:** LLM-heavy attack generation/setup may be heavier than this repo’s lightweight augmentation stack.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **91/100**

### Paper B
- **Citation:** Park, S., Han, S., Xie, X., Lee, J.-G., & Cha, M. (2025). *Adversarial Style Augmentation via Large Language Model for Robust Fake News Detection*. arXiv:2406.11260 (WWW 2025).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11260
- **Core method:** Uses LLM-generated style-conversion adversarial prompts (AdStyle) to augment training with difficult style variants while preserving core claim content.
- **Assumptions:** Style artifacts are major non-robust features; adversarial style diversity during training improves resilience to style-conversion attacks.
- **Strengths:** Strong fit for this project’s `style_invariance`; practical training-time augmentation perspective.
- **Limitations:** Reliance on prompt/LLM generation quality; portability to non-LLM augmentation pipelines needs careful approximation.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **89/100**

### Paper C
- **Citation:** Flores, L. J. Y., & Hao, Y. (2022). *An Adversarial Benchmark for Fake News Detection Models*. arXiv:2201.00912.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00912
- **Core method:** Proposes adversarial benchmark slices targeting compositional semantics, lexical relations, and modifier sensitivity, showing brittleness of fine-tuned BERT detectors.
- **Assumptions:** Small lexical/compositional edits can break fake-news decisions without true factual changes.
- **Strengths:** Very actionable for lexical mHC-lite safety constraints and evaluation slicing; directly motivates protected pivot operators.
- **Limitations:** Benchmark-focused (not a training method); older than A/B but still highly relevant to lexical robustness diagnostics.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **87/100**

## 2) Concise synthesis vs current fake_news_project methods
- The project’s current strategy lock already maps well to paper directions: lexical consistency (`lexical_mhc_lite`) + style/sentiment invariance.
- AdSent and AdStyle both reinforce one key point: robustness improves when augmentation is diverse but label/claim preserving.
- The adversarial benchmark paper strengthens the case for conservative lexical guards around compositional certainty operators (modals/functional pivots), which is directly actionable in mHC-lite augmentation.

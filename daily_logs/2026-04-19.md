## New Papers (2-3)

### 1) FactGuard (AAAI 2026)
- **Citation:** He, J., Zhang, H., Xiao, Y., Guo, W., Yao, S., & Liu, R. (2025). *FactGuard: Event-Centric and Commonsense-Guided Fake News Detection*. arXiv:2511.10281 (accepted AAAI 2026).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10281
- **Core method:** Event-centric extraction + contradiction/ambiguity-aware reasoning, with dynamic LLM advice and distilled lightweight model for deployment.
- **Assumptions:** Style imitation is a major attack path; event/content signals are more robust than superficial publisher style.
- **Strengths:** Strong robustness framing against style mimicry; practical distillation angle.
- **Limitations:** LLM-advice components are heavier than this repo’s lightweight augmentation/training design.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **86/100**

### 2) Style-News (EACL 2024)
- **Citation:** Wang, W.-Y., Chang, Y.-C., & Peng, W.-C. (2024). *Style-News: Incorporating Stylized News Generation and Adversarial Verification for Neural Fake News Detection*. EACL 2024.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15509
- **Core method:** Adversarial stylized-news generation conditioned on publisher template/style, with discriminators to detect style/source manipulation.
- **Assumptions:** Publisher/style signals are exploitable shortcuts; controlled style adversaries improve detector robustness.
- **Strengths:** Directly relevant to `style_invariance`; clear attack-defense style framing.
- **Limitations:** Focuses on neural-generated fake news and publisher metadata setup not fully mirrored in current pipeline.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **90/100**

### 3) Automatic Fake News Detection: Fact-checking or Gut-checking? (FEVER Workshop 2022)
- **Citation:** Kelk, I., Basseri, B., Lee, W. Y., Qiu, R., & Tanner, C. (2022). *Automatic Fake News Detection: Are current models "fact-checking" or "gut-checking"?* FEVER Workshop 2022.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07229
- **Core method:** Neutralizes manipulable style/emotion/POS cues to test whether models truly use claim-evidence reasoning vs shortcut signals.
- **Assumptions:** Many fake-news detectors exploit sentiment/style shortcuts rather than factual consistency.
- **Strengths:** Very actionable for invariance design; gives concrete cue categories to suppress in augmentation.
- **Limitations:** Benchmark/study framing rather than a drop-in training algorithm.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **88/100**

## Concise Synthesis vs Current fake_news_project Methods
- Current project already aligns with these papers via explicit invariance strategies (`style_invariance`, `sentiment_invariance`) and mHC-lite lexical consistency.
- Main gap highlighted by today’s papers: **wire/publisher wrapper artifacts** (datelines, source prefixes) can act as shortcuts, even when lexical perturbation is conservative.
- Practical implication: strengthen `style_reframe_simple` so style-invariance training views remove additional outlet/dateline wrappers while preserving claim content.

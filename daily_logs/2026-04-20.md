## New Papers (2-3, in-scope)

### 1) Effect of Text Augmentation and Adversarial Training on Fake News Detection
- **Citation:** Alsmadi, I., et al. (2024). *Effect of Text Augmentation and Adversarial Training on Fake News Detection*. **IEEE Transactions on Computational Social Systems**. DOI: 10.1109/TCSS.2023.3344597.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSS.2023.3344597
- **Core method:** Uses text augmentation both as robustness-improving training data and as adversarial attack generation; then applies adversarial training to reduce attack success.
- **Assumptions:** (i) augmentation can expose model brittleness, (ii) adversarially trained models learn more stable decision boundaries for fake-news text.
- **Strengths:** Directly fake-news-specific robustness framing; reports practical gains and attack-resistance improvements.
- **Limitations:** Attack/augmentation design details may be dataset-specific; less explicit decomposition by lexical vs style vs sentiment channels.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **90/100**

### 2) Improving fake news detection with domain-adversarial and graph-attention neural network
- **Citation:** Hu, L., Yang, T., Zhang, L., Zhong, W., Tang, D., Shi, C., Duan, N., Zhou, M. (2021). *Improving fake news detection with domain-adversarial and graph-attention neural network*. **Decision Support Systems**, 151, 113633. DOI: 10.1016/j.dss.2021.113633.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dss.2021.113633
- **Core method:** Domain-adversarial learning + graph attention for cross-domain fake-news detection, aiming to suppress domain/style shortcut dependence.
- **Assumptions:** Robustness failures are often due to domain/style shift; domain-invariant representations improve transfer to unseen events/topics.
- **Strengths:** Strong robustness-to-shift perspective aligned with style invariance goals.
- **Limitations:** Heavier architecture than this lightweight repository; not focused on controlled lexical/sentiment perturbation operators.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **82/100**

### 3) Adversarial Training with Fast Gradient Projection Method against Synonym Substitution based Text Attacks
- **Citation:** Huang, Y., Zheng, S., Gao, Y., Cheng, J., Yang, Y. (2021). *Adversarial Training with Fast Gradient Projection Method against Synonym Substitution based Text Attacks*. **AAAI**. DOI: 10.1609/aaai.v35i16.17648.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i16.17648
- **Core method:** Proposes FGPM, a fast synonym-substitution attack/training pipeline, making adversarial training practical for lexical substitution threats.
- **Assumptions:** Fast, constrained lexical adversaries can provide robust gradients without excessive compute.
- **Strengths:** Highly relevant to `lexical_mhc_lite`; reinforces importance of perturbation-budget control and lexical constraints.
- **Limitations:** General text classification focus (not fake-news-specific); may require additional adaptation for claim-preservation constraints.
- **Worth-Reading (this project):** **88/100**

## Concise Synthesis vs Current fake_news_project Methods
- Current project already operationalizes channel-specific robustness (`lexical_mhc_lite`, `style_invariance`, `sentiment_invariance`) with lightweight augmenters.
- Today’s papers collectively reinforce one design principle: **robustness improves when perturbation strength is explicit and controlled** (too weak = little robustness signal, too strong = semantic drift/noisy invariance).
- The most actionable gap in current code was strict lexical budget semantics: `budget_ratio=0` should mean guaranteed no lexical perturbation for clean/noise-sensitive ablations and schedule warm-ups.
